# Social Media Campaign for Programs

A repeatable, framework-first method for promoting any "program" — an event, a product/software launch, a course/cohort, a church service/crusade, a recurring series — across social media. The same skeleton adapts to any program type via the **Program-Type Playbook** section at the bottom.

## When to use

- Tutu asks to "promote", "launch", "drive a campaign for", or "get signups/attendance/awareness" for anything with a date, a signup link, or a launch moment.
- Planning pre-launch buzz, launch-day coverage, or post-launch sustain.
- Setting up a content calendar, caption templates, or a KPI dashboard for a program.

## Mental model (read first)

1. **A campaign is a funnel, not a feed.** Every post maps to Reach → Act → Convert → Engage (RACE). If a post serves no stage, cut it.
2. **Programs have a lifecycle.** Pre-launch builds anticipation → launch converts → post-launch sustains and repurposes. Content cadence and tone shift per phase.
3. **80/20 content mix.** ~80% of posts educate/entertain/deliver value; ~20% promote with a clear CTA. Promotion-heavy feeds get muted (Source: Hootsuite/SocialPilot convention).
4. **Vanity vs. actionable metrics.** Followers/likes are ego fuel. Track what ties to the goal: click-through, signups, registrations, attendance, conversions (Sources: Sprout Social, AgencyAnalytics).
5. **Process over individual-post performance.** Judge the campaign in aggregate (reach, engagement rate, conversion rate) over its lifecycle, not by any single post's numbers.

## Phase 0 — Program intake (30 min)

Before touching a platform, answer these in `templates/campaign-brief.md`:

1. **Program:** one-line description. What is it, exactly?
2. **The one goal:** pick ONE primary objective (signups / attendance / downloads / awareness). Secondary goals come after.
3. **Audience:** who must show up or sign up? (age, location, interest, pain point)
4. **Offer / hook:** why should they care? The single most compelling reason.
5. **Date & timeline:** launch/event date, and how many weeks of runway remain.
6. **Assets:** what already exists (logo, photos, video, testimonials, speaker/guest names)?
7. **Conversion destination:** where do people go? (registration form, WhatsApp, ticket link, app store)
8. **Budget:** organic-only, or is there a paid-boost budget? How much?
9. **Owner:** who replies to comments/DMs, and how fast?

If the goal or audience is fuzzy, stop and clarify with Tutu before producing content. Content without a defined goal is decoration.

## Phase 1 — Strategy (SOSTAC-lite)

Use the SOSTAC skeleton (Situation, Objectives, Strategy, Tactics, Action, Control) compressed to what matters for a single program:

**Situation** — 3–5 bullets:
- Where does the audience already hang out online? (don't guess — check where the last similar program got traction)
- What's the existing channel state: account reach, follower base, past engagement?
- Who else is promoting a competing program? What are they doing well/poorly?

**Objectives** — SMART, tied to the one goal:
- Bad: "get more awareness."
- Good: "200 registrations by launch day" / "150 event check-ins" / "1,000 installs in 30 days."
- Every objective needs a number, a deadline, and a way to measure it (link click, form submit, ticket scan).

**Strategy** — the positioning in one sentence: "We will [reach audience] via [channels] by [content angle], converting them at [destination]."

**Tactics** — the channel + content decisions below (Phases 2–4).

**Action & Control** — the calendar (who posts what, when) and the KPI dashboard (Phase 6).

### Channel selection (pick 2–3, don't spray everywhere)

Choose by audience + goal, not by habit. General 2025/2026 landscape (verify against current data when it matters — platform facts drift):

| Channel | Best for | Signal to watch |
|---|---|---|
| Facebook | Community, events, 25–55+, church/general audiences, Groups | Event responses, shares |
| Instagram | Visual, 18–45, brand storytelling, Reels discovery | Saves, shares, profile taps |
| TikTok | 13–35 reach, short-form virality, youth programs | Watch time, completes |
| LinkedIn | Professional/B2B, courses, training, corporate programs | Clicks, profile views |
| YouTube | Long-form, replays, tutorials, sermon/worship recordings | Watch time, subs |
| X/Twitter | Real-time, announcements, niche/tech audiences | Reposts, replies |
| WhatsApp (Business + broadcast lists + Status) | **Direct/reminder channel — huge in Ghana/West Africa**, RSVPs, follow-ups, group mobilisation | Replies, RSVP confirmations |

Ghana/regional note: WhatsApp and Facebook carry disproportionate weight for local church, community, and event programs; TikTok/Instagram for youth; LinkedIn for professional/training programs. A WhatsApp broadcast list or group is often the highest-converting "reminder" layer — use it to drive attendance, not as the only channel.

## Phase 2 — Content system

### Content pillars (3–5, reusable themes)

Pick pillars that map to the program. Examples: *Educate* (why this matters), *Behind-the-scenes* (build/planning/rehearsal), *Social proof* (testimonials, past results, speaker credibility), *Announce/Urgency* (dates, deadlines, countdown), *Entertain/Relate* (memes, relatable moments).

Every post is tagged to a pillar + a RACE stage. This keeps the mix honest and prevents an all-promo feed.

### 80/20 rule (operationalised)

Plan 4 value-led posts for every 1 promotional post. During launch week the ratio can invert to ~50/50; over the full campaign it should land near 80/20.

### Content calendar

Use `templates/content-calendar.md`. Columns: Date, Channel, Pillar, RACE stage, Format (static/carousel/Reel/video/story), Copy/CTA, Asset link, Status.

Batch-produce in weekly sprints; schedule ahead. Consistency beats bursts.

## Phase 3 — Production

### Copy — use a formula, then stack them

- **AIDA** (Attention → Interest → Desire → Action): hook with a headline, build interest with facts, create desire with the benefit/outcome, close with a CTA.
- **PAS** (Problem → Agitate → Solution): name the pain, amplify the cost of ignoring it, present the program as the fix. Best when the program solves a felt problem (course, tool, training).
- **BAB** (Before → After → Bridge): paint the current state, the transformed state, and the program as the bridge. Best for aspirational outcomes (weight, skills, career, faith growth).
- **4 C's** (Clear, Concise, Compelling, Credible): a quality gate, not a structure — run every caption through it.

Hook formulas (first line decides scroll-past): a bold claim, a surprising stat, a question, a contradiction ("Why most X fail…"), a deadline ("Only 3 days left").

CTA discipline: one CTA per post. "Register → link in bio", "Comment 'SAVE' and we'll DM the details", "Tap to get tickets". No double CTAs.

### Hashtag counts (current guidance — re-verify, these shift)

- Instagram: 3–5 (hard 5-hashtag cap since Dec 2025). Hashtags now act as content-classification signals, not reach drivers.
- TikTok / LinkedIn / YouTube / Threads / Pinterest: 3–5 targeted tags.
- X/Twitter: 1–2 (posts with 2+ see engagement drops).
- Facebook: hashtags barely matter; skip or use 1–2.

Use a mix: 1–2 broad category tags + 2–3 niche/program-specific tags.

### Visuals

Consistent template per campaign (same fonts/colors) = recognisability. Carousels for value/how-to; short vertical video (Reels/TikTok) for reach; stories for reminders and urgency. Square/static is the weakest format for reach now — default to video/carousel unless a static is clearly right.

### AI-assisted workflow (your stack: ChatGPT-class LLM + Canva)

1. LLM generates 10 caption drafts from one brief (paste the campaign-brief + pillar + formula).
2. LLM turns a long asset (blog, sermon, doc) into 5–8 short posts.
3. Canva (or LLM image gen) for carousels/thumbnails from the caption angle.
4. **Human pass is mandatory** — edit for voice, factual accuracy, and to strip AI-tells. Never ship raw LLM copy; it reads generic and kills engagement.

## Phase 4 — Execution & launch timeline

### Standard T-minus schedule (compress if runway is short)

- **T-4 to T-3 weeks — Teaser:** announce "something is coming", save-the-date, behind-the-scenes prep. Goal: seed anticipation, no hard CTA yet.
- **T-2 weeks — Value + soft pitch:** educate on the topic, share past results/testimonials, open early registration, introduce the people behind it. Begin countdown.
- **T-1 week — Urgency:** FAQs, speaker/guest spotlights, deadline reminders, "last chance" framing, social proof push.
- **Launch/event day — Convert:** pinned announcement post, live stories/coverage, link-in-bio refresh, real-time replies, reshare attendee posts.
- **Post-launch — Sustain:** recap content, thank-you posts, replay/recording (if applicable), testimonial harvest, repurpose best content for evergreen value.

### Scheduling

Use a scheduler (Buffer, Hootsuite, Later, Publer — pick one, own it). Schedule the batch, then spend live time on engagement, not posting. Post at audience-active windows (check platform analytics, not generic "best time" blogs).

### Paid amplification (only if budget exists)

- **Organic first.** Paid amplifies what already resonates; it does not rescue weak creative. Boost only the posts that already outperform organically.
- If boosting: define the objective (traffic, conversions, event responses), set a per-day budget, target the program's actual audience, and track ROAS/CPC — never boost "for visibility" without a tracked destination.

## Phase 5 — Engagement & community

- Reply to every comment/DM during launch week within hours; promptness signals a live, human program.
- Seed the conversation: ask a question in captions, reply to early comments to lift the thread.
- WhatsApp/Status and broadcast lists for direct reminders (especially Ghana/local programs).

### Crisis & negative comments

- **Never disable comments** as a first resort — it amplifies the problem and looks like hiding (Source: Brandwatch).
- Pre-write response templates for the 3–5 most likely complaints.
- Escalation path: routine replies → community manager → program owner → legal/leadership for serious issues.
- Detection trigger (Source: Sprinklr): a 3x mention spike with a 20% sentiment drop within an hour = escalate for review immediately.
- If the program made a real mistake, apologise plainly and fast — an early apology defuses most situations.
- Ignore pure trolling (don't feed it); answer genuine concerns with facts and empathy.

## Phase 6 — Measurement & iteration

Track against the six KPI categories (Source: Hootsuite): engagement, awareness, conversions, ROI, customer care, content performance.

Minimum viable dashboard (`templates/kpi-dashboard.md`), chosen by goal:
- **Conversions** (primary for signups/attendance/sales): clicks, signups, registrations, cost-per-conversion.
- **Awareness**: reach, impressions, follower growth (secondary).
- **Engagement**: engagement rate = (likes+comments+shares+saves) / reach — the quality signal.
- **ROI**: revenue or attendance / spend (if paid).

Review cadence: quick check every 3–4 days during the campaign; full retro at the end. Iterate: double down on the pillar/format/angle that over-performs; kill what under-performs; carry learnings into the next program.

## Program-Type Playbook (adapt the skeleton)

**Event (conference, concert, crusade, service):** lead with save-the-date → speaker/lineup reveals → venue/logistics info → live coverage → recap + next-event seed. Facebook Events + WhatsApp broadcast are the conversion workhorses.

**Product / software launch:** tease the problem → demo/build-in-public → beta/waitlist → launch day → changelog + testimonials. Target dev/tech communities (X, Reddit, LinkedIn, Product Hunt for the right products). Social proof (screenshots, reviews) outweighs claims.

**Course / training / cohort:** sell the outcome, not the syllabus. Before/After framing (BAB), student testimonials, "who this is for" clarity, enrollment deadline urgency. LinkedIn + Instagram + WhatsApp for professional audiences.

**Church program / recurring service:** consistency over virality. Weekly pillars (sermon clips, worship moments, testimonies, announcements), strong WhatsApp/Status layer, Facebook community. Sermon/worship short clips are the highest-reuse asset — clip and repurpose relentlessly.

**Webinar / online program:** registration is the whole game. Multiple registration CTAs across the timeline, reminder sequence (email + WhatsApp), "can't attend live? get the replay" to remove the objection.

## Pitfalls

- **All-promo feed** — breaks the 80/20 rule; audience tunes out. Fix with pillars.
- **No single goal** — "awareness" + "signups" + "sales" at once = none measured. Pick one primary.
- **Posting without a destination** — great post, nowhere to convert. Every promo post needs a link.
- **Vanity-metric chasing** — optimising for likes while signups stall. Watch conversion KPIs.
- **Inconsistent posting** — a burst then silence. Batch + schedule.
- **Ignoring comments/DMs** — kills the algorithm signal and the community. Reply fast.
- **One-size caption across platforms** — X needs brevity, LinkedIn professionalism, TikTok native tone. Rewrite per platform.
- **Shipping raw AI copy** — generic, gets ignored. Human edit always.
- **No hashtag discipline** — 30 hashtags on Instagram (against the 5-cap) reads as spam.
- **No retros** — repeating the same under-performing format next campaign. End with a retro.

## Verification checklist (campaign is "done right" when…)

- [ ] Campaign brief filled: one goal, defined audience, dated objective, conversion destination.
- [ ] 2–3 channels chosen with a reason tied to the audience.
- [ ] Content calendar populated ≥2 weeks out, tagged pillar + RACE stage.
- [ ] Mix near 80/20 over the full timeline.
- [ ] Every promo post has exactly one CTA + a working link.
- [ ] Captions pass the 4 C's and use a named formula.
- [ ] Hashtag counts match per-platform guidance.
- [ ] KPI dashboard set up and baselined (reach/engagement/conversions) before launch.
- [ ] Reply/ownership assigned; crisis templates pre-written.
- [ ] Retro scheduled for post-launch.
